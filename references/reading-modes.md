# Reading depth and optional lenses

Depth controls how much of the paper is read. Lens controls which extra questions are emphasized. Do not combine them into an automatic escalation ladder.

| Request | Depth | Reader-facing result |
|---|---|---|
| “Read this paper”, “仔细阅读”, “make a mind map” | DEEP READ (default) | Full reading atlas: whole → path → mechanism → experiments → synthesis |
| “What is this about?”, “worth reading?” | SCAN | Provisional global map, principal result, relevance if context exists |
| “Review / find weaknesses / do claims hold?” | DEEP READ + REVIEW | Full atlas, followed by evidence and fairness audit |
| “Compare with my work / reproduce / next experiments” | DEEP READ + RESEARCH | Full atlas, followed by grounded comparison, implementation details, or research card |

A user-supplied paper and a request to read it are sufficient intent for DEEP READ. Do not invent a purpose such as “judge how many claims are supported,” a research project, or a relevance threshold. Ask only if a missing choice would materially change the deliverable and cannot be inferred.

## Passes

1. **Orientation:** title, abstract, introduction, central figures, conclusion, major results. Draft a provisional map.
2. **Reconstruction:** read the full relevant method/argument and experiments, including figure captions, table notes, definitions, and key appendix material. Revise the map wherever the source contradicts it.
3. **Explanation:** make the atlas readable from overview to detail. Connect diagram elements to narrative and original source locations.
4. **Optional audit or application:** inspect controls, fairness, uncertainty, reproduction, or research implications according to the selected lens.

SCAN can end after orientation. DEEP READ completes reconstruction and explanation even if the reader did not give an external goal. A paper can be interesting without being relevant to an active project.
